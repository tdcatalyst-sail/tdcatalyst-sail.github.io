A note on this tool

This is a demonstration of a transformation simulation: a deliberately simplified model, not a forecast of your organization. Its purpose is to make the dynamics of transformation work visible: how interventions, their sequence, and their timing compound or stall, and how that plays out across a whole population over time. Use it to build intuition for the shape of the work, not to predict a number.

    What to get right, in order

    Left: the groundwork that lets the next step land. Right: how each move drives real outcomes (speed, quality, results) versus just widening reach. Run a play out of order, or before its proof exists, and the chain is flagged.

    Illustrative model, not a forecast, built to make the dynamics visible. What it isolates: the human sequencing of a transformation: who moves first, how peer proof gates the next play, the dip before the payoff, why executive sponsorship is the gate nothing crosses without, and why adoption without capability is usage but not value. It renders the logic of the Adoption Matrix. What it assumes, and where the harder work lives: active resistance and backlash, the tool's real relative advantage and fit for each role, deep role-based capability-building, and the reinforcement systems that keep the change from decaying. The model treats these as given; they rarely are. Those, with your actual data, are what the Adoption Reset surfaces and builds. The tool is the map; the terrain is the engagement.

    The lesson in the model

    Order is the intervention.

    Put the laggard path or the skeptic reframe in week one and watch it barely move the line. There's no peer proof yet for it to stand on. Seed proof with your innovators first, equip managers early to amplify everything after, and the same plays compound instead of fizzling. None of it runs itself: your team clears the time, has the conversations, and does the groundwork. The sequencing judgment (which cell, which play, in which order) is what separates a rollout that holds from one that decays. That's the part we do together.
