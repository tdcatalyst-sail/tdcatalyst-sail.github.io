Why rollouts stall

Stalled adoption is an organizational problem, not a technical one.

MIT reports that 95% of enterprise GenAI pilots produce no measurable business impact. The platform is rarely the reason. Whether the rollout is Copilot, Gemini, or a custom deployment, the same three gaps appear in nearly every stalled program.

01

Workflow integration

The tool runs alongside the process it was meant to change, so usage stays optional. Optional usage decays quarter over quarter.

02

Manager reinforcement

Frontline managers were trained, not equipped. Nothing in their operating rhythm requires the new way of working, so the old way persists.

03

Value measurement

No shared measure ties usage to an operating result. The program cannot show ROI, and the sponsor cannot defend the next phase.

The deliverable

A written decision, not a deck.

The Recovery Verdict is a board-ready document: the assessment, the decision, and the plan. Specific enough to act on, and written to hold up when forwarded to someone who was not in the room.

  • Root cause, identified. Why adoption stalled, with the evidence from your own usage data and interviews.
  • Scale, repair, or stop. A clear decision on each workstream, with the reasoning attached.
  • A 90-day recovery plan. Sequenced actions, each with a named owner.
  • One success metric. The measure that will show it working, who reports it, and how often.
The process

Ten business days, structured.

  1. 1–3Stakeholder interviews

    Five to eight conversations: the executive sponsor, the program owner, function leads, and frontline managers.

  2. 4–7Evidence review

    Usage and license data, workflow documentation, program reporting, and vendor metrics.

  3. 8Executive working session

    Findings presented to the decision-makers before anything is finalized.

  4. 10The Recovery Verdict

    Delivered in writing, on day ten.
