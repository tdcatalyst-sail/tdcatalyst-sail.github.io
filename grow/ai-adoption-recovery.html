Where you are starting from

Four situations, one discipline: the work has to change, and someone has to own the change.

Enterprise AI rarely fails loudly. People run the tool in parallel with the old way, the value is never clearly architected, and usage quietly fades. MIT found 95% of enterprise GenAI pilots show no measurable P&L impact, and adoption typically plateaus at 15–30% after launch. The Review meets you in whichever of these four situations you are in.

Stalled platform

The department platform launched. Usage plateaued.

An enterprise platform (talent, service, finance, operations) licensed, launched, and stuck. Deployment is a project plan; adoption is a population of people choosing, every day, to work differently. The recovery grows use of the compounding features the platform was bought for, past log-in counts, through an explicit workflow redesign: map the task, name the augmented steps, add the new steps, build the capability, and hold it in the team cadence.

Delivery lands inside the suites your teams already live in, wired to the workflows, and the plan is measured on feature-depth per workflow, never on log-ins.

Frontier-lab seatsFlagship

Everyone got a seat. Now every role has to change.

Enterprise seats from the frontier labs (Anthropic Claude, OpenAI ChatGPT, Microsoft Copilot, Google Gemini) put a general-purpose capability in every role at once. Log-ins arrive on day one; the actual deployment is redrawing who does what. Every task in a role gets sorted by four plain questions into three lanes: human-led, shared with AI, or handed to AI, and the freed capacity gets pointed at named work.

This is the engagement the practice is built around: AI work redesign, role by role, with the sorting logic applied live in the working sessions. The intervention library below carries the change side.

The forty pilots

Forty pilots are live, and every platform now sells agents.

The agentic push left a pilot landscape nobody governs, and it is compounding: every incumbent platform in your stack is asking for credits to test its new agents. That is the real arbitrage on the table. The Review maps every pilot and every vendor-agent offer against explicit criteria (value evidence, workflow embed, named sponsor, cost to serve) and delivers a decision on each line: scale, institutionalize, or stop.

Written to survive the sponsor conversation and the renewal negotiation.

Starting 0 → 1

A handful of people use AI. Some of them are hiding it.

Leadership has barely touched the tools, no platform is chosen, no use policy exists, and the only number anyone can report is log-ins (log-ins as the headline metric are the tell). The 0→1 arc runs on cadence: leadership aligns first and writes the charter and use policy, teams activate on real work in builder events, and the new way gets embedded in policy, criteria, and rituals.

The first signals decide the path: a third of employees read AI as replacement, 62% as augmentation, and trust in leadership's intent moves that belief more than any training program (BetterUp).
