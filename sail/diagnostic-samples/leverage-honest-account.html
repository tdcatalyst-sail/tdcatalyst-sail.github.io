Leverage Inventory: The Honest Account

Section 03 of 04, companion page to the inventory table · Illustrative sample

The inventory table records what is present. This page records the rest, because the honest accounting is the part of the diagnostic that earns its keep.

What is absent

Three capabilities are missing outright. The first is sensing infrastructure. No named owner reads weak signals, no time is protected for it, no escalation path exists, and no cost threshold has been set for deciding whether a response is warranted. Detection currently happens by accident. The second is the transforming muscle: the capacity to reconfigure resources and structures to deliver on what was seized. Its absence shows up as pilots that hold their numbers without changing how the work is organized. The third is a shared name for the transition itself. The symptoms of the unmarked passage were visible on the walk: vocabulary that reverts by Monday morning regardless of what the offsite decided, and different registers when different leaders describe the same change.

What is misallocated

Two resources are in the wrong place. Executive attention is the first. The calendar is fully committed to operational meetings, which means the organization's most senior reader of signals has no altitude from which to read. The second is the training budget, currently aimed at tool skills ahead of role redesign. The sequence matters: operating model first, capability second. Retraining people into unredesigned roles produces a faster version of the same workflow.

Why absence is the useful finding

A weak capability invites incremental fixes. An absent one has to be designed, given an owner, and built, which is a different kind of work and a more honest starting point. Each absent item above appears in the 90-day roadmap as a build, with a named owner and a decision point. Each misallocated item appears as a reallocation the executive can make in the first two weeks.
