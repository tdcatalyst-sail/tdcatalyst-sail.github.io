TDcatalyst Advise · For founders moving upmarket

Your product is ready for the enterprise. Is your company?

The motion that got you to a few million in ARR is not the motion that wins a Fortune 100 account. Enterprise buyers evaluate an AI product through procurement, security, the works council, the CHRO's risk calculus, and the question no demo answers: what happens to our people when this works? TDcatalyst Advise is a standing advisory relationship for founders crossing that line — built by someone who sat on the other side of the table and built the enterprise practice you are trying to sell into.

The offer

A standing relationship, not a deliverable.

Advise is structured as a monthly retainer or an advisory-board seat. The form differs; the substance is the same — operator judgment on call as the enterprise motion takes shape.

01

Deal and account counsel

Live support on enterprise opportunities: stakeholder mapping, the buyer's actual decision process, navigating procurement and security review, and reading the difference between a champion and a sponsor before the quarter depends on it.

02

Enterprise readiness

The company the enterprise expects to meet: packaging and pricing for committee-based buying, an adoption and value story that survives the pilot, customer success that holds a renewal, and references that compound. Built before the deal exposes their absence.

03

Board-level perspective

For the advisory-board form: a standing voice in quarterly strategy on the upmarket transition — sequencing, hiring the first enterprise roles, where the roadmap meets the buyer's workflow, and what the move costs the culture. Candid, in writing when it matters.

Why this advisor

The view from the other side of the table.

Tom Delaporte built an enterprise customer practice at an AI company from a standing start into an eight-figure ARR business, across Fortune 100 and public-sector accounts. Before that he led organizational design and change engagements at a global consulting firm, and ran HR and transformation on the operator side across five countries. He has been the person your champion has to convince — and the person who decided whether the renewal happened.

That is the asymmetry Advise corrects. Most go-to-market advice comes from people who have sold to the enterprise. This is counsel from someone who has also bought, deployed, and run AI inside it — who knows where adoption actually breaks after the contract is signed, because that is where the renewal is decided.

Who it's for

Founders at the upmarket inflection.

The fit is a founder or founding team with an AI product that has real traction — and the first enterprise logos in the pipeline, or the ambition to put them there. Especially products that touch the workforce: talent, HR technology, productivity, agentic tools that change how people work. That is the buying center Tom knows best, and the one where the people question decides the deal.

Advise takes a small number of companies at a time, and never two that compete.
